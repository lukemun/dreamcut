# Cross into Negative

Template `FX-CROSS-NEGATIVE` (Properties → Templates). Over shot A, a cross-shaped window
opens onto shot B shown as a black-and-white negative: a bar wipes in from the right,
a bar drops from the top, then both widen until the negative fills the frame and shot B
plays on as a negative. Recreated from a breakdown reel by @willrvail of a video by
@nvision.media (instagram.com/reel/Deb-VcJRpxp); no reference pixels are reused.

## Inputs

| Role | Required | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shot A | Yes | The outgoing shot, at least 1.6 s before the cut. |
| Shot B | Yes | The incoming shot. Strong light and shadow read best as a negative. |

The agent prepares a white plate (one 1920×1080 white PNG, imported once) for the
inversion. Nothing is generated by a model.

## Build

Let the cut from A to B be at `C`. With `d` the gap between bars (demo 0.4 s):

1. Grade shot B (an existing clip, so a `props` operation): `grade_saturation: 0`,
   `grade_contrast: 1.3`.
2. Add a full-frame white still over shot B for its whole length with
   `media_blend_mode: "difference"`. White difference inverts everything below it.
3. Bar across: a graded copy of shot B (source from its first second) starting at
   `C − 1.6` for 1.6 s, plus a white difference still with the same timing, each with
   `mask_inset: [0.38, 0, 0.5, 0.95]` and
   `mask_inset_keyframes: [{t:0,v:[0.38,0,0.5,0.95],ease:"easeOut"},{t:0.25,v:[0.38,0,0.5,0],ease:"hold"},{t:1,v:[0.38,0,0.5,0],ease:"easeIn"},{t:1.5,v:[0,0,0,0]}]`.
4. Bar down: the same pair starting at `C − 1.6 + d`, lasting `1.6 − d`, with
   `mask_inset: [0, 0.47, 0.95, 0.47]` and keyframes opening down over 0.25 s, holding,
   then widening to `[0,0,0,0]` so both bars fill the frame at `C − 0.1`.
5. Each bar copy sits on its own lane directly below its white still; register every
   lane with `set_lane_order` (top-first: bar down negative, bar down, bar across
   negative, bar across, full-frame negative, shots).
6. Add a `timing_controls` spacing entry binding both bar-down operations'
   `timeline_start` to `C − 1.6 + d` so the gap is one dial.
7. Replay the card. Check the first bar, the cross, the full negative and the cut.

## Controls on the proposal card

Second bar after (the gap between bars), Contrast of shot B, and the bar copies'
Source in. Bar placement and widening keyframes stay in the proposal.

## Timing (recreation)

From the recreation: first bar
at `C − 1.6 s` opening over 0.25 s, second bar 0.4 s later, both widening from
`C − 0.6 s` to fill the frame at `C − 0.1 s`. The reference breaks into blocks as it
opens; this construction widens smoothly.

## Limits

- Smooth widening, not the reference's block break-up.
- Bar keyframes are not dials on the card yet.
- The bar positions are fractions of a 16:9 frame; a 9:16 version needs its own bar
  positions.
